03 What does it feel like?

Your words.
A clearer starting point.

You don’t need footwear expertise. Describe the sensation you notice, without having to decide what is causing it.

  • 01

    Slipping.

    Your heel lifts as you walk, or your foot moves forward inside the shoe.

  • 02

    Squeezing.

    Your toes feel crowded, or you notice pressure across the front of your foot.

  • 03

    Tightness.

    The top of your foot feels pressed by the laces or the upper.

  • 04

    Rubbing.

    A part of the shoe repeatedly catches or rubs against your foot.

04 When does it happen?

The moment matters, too.

Tell Fit Check when discomfort shows up during wear. Timing adds context to the experience you describe.

  1. 01

    When you put them on.

    Something feels uncomfortable from the start.

  2. 02

    As you move.

    You notice the issue while walking in the pair.

  3. 03

    As wear continues.

    The discomfort develops or changes while you wear them.

These are examples of wear context, not timing thresholds or a reason to keep wearing an uncomfortable pair.

08 Shoe anatomy / Glossary

Know the shoe.
Understand the words.

A little footwear vocabulary can make fit guidance easier to follow. Open a term for a plain-language explanation.

Explore Learn ↗
Toe box

The front part of the shoe that surrounds your toes.

Heel counter

The reinforcement around the back of the shoe, behind and beside your heel.

Vamp

The part of the upper that covers the top of the front of your foot.

Eyelets

The holes or loops that the shoelaces pass through.

Shoe last

The form a shoe is made around. It helps define the shoe’s shape.

09 A clear boundary

Shoe guidance.
With limits.

Fit Check provides educational guidance about discomfort with a specific pair. It does not diagnose a condition or replace professional care.

Safety context is part of the check. Do not use footwear adjustments as a reason to push through pain or ignore symptoms that concern you. Seek professional advice when needed.
